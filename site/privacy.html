The design principle

Minimum necessary exposure.

Every field belongs on exactly one side of this line, and the split is decided at design time rather than left to runtime discretion.

🌐 Public — because verification matters
  • Market prices and their FTSOv2 timestamps
  • Collateral amount held by the vault
  • The resulting score and LTV cap
  • Attestation signature and expiry
  • Enclave image hash and registered signer set
🔒 Private — because confidentiality matters
  • Account age and full transaction history
  • Off-chain reserves and portfolio size
  • Repayment record and prior liquidations
  • Personal risk thresholds and strategy
  • Every intermediate model feature
🔐

Why a TEE is unavoidable

The borrower holds the data. The lending contract must act on a conclusion drawn from it. Two parties, opposing incentives, no trusted middle. Delete the enclave and the product doesn't degrade — it ceases to exist.

#️⃣

The model itself is attested

An extension's on-chain code version is the hash of its Docker image. The operator cannot silently swap in a biased model, because the hash would change and the registry would reject the signature.

👁️

What an attacker sees

A ciphertext blob of predictable length, a timestamp, and a score. Not enough to invert the inputs — and the score alone is something the borrower chose to disclose.

Honest accounting

What this does not protect against.

A privacy claim is only worth as much as its stated limits, so here are ours.

Hardware trust

TEE guarantees rest on the silicon vendor's attestation chain. A verified break in that chain breaks this. We inherit that assumption rather than solving it — as does every confidential-computing system in production today.

Metadata leakage

The fact that you requested a score, and roughly when, is public. The payload is padded so its length reveals little, but timing analysis remains a real avenue.

The score is a disclosure

A published PRIME tier tells observers something about you. It's a single bounded fact you consciously chose to reveal, which is the point — but it isn't zero disclosure, and we won't claim it is.
