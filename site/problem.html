Why it stays broken

Both existing answers are bad.

This isn't an oversight nobody noticed. It's a genuine dilemma, and the two available escapes each destroy something essential.

📢

Option A — publish everything

Put your balance sheet on a public chain and every competitor, front-runner and liquidation bot can read your position in real time. Your borrowing capacity becomes a map of exactly where to squeeze you.

Nobody does this, and they're right not to.
🏦

Option B — trust a credit bureau

Hand your financial history to a centralised scorer and you've rebuilt TradFi: an opaque database, an unauditable model, a single breach away from catastrophe, and a gatekeeper who can quietly decide you're not worth lending to.

Solves privacy from the public, not from the scorer.
The gap

The borrower holds data the lender needs. The lender must act on a conclusion drawn from that data. Neither party can safely hand the other what they have — so the protocol defaults to treating everyone like the riskiest possible counterparty. That default is the tax every honest borrower pays.
