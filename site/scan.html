What goes wrong

Four ways people lose money signing one transaction.

None of these are exotic. They're the ordinary failure modes of an interface that shows you a hex string and asks you to trust it.

◱

Unlimited approvals

You approve a token spend once. The allowance never expires, and months later the contract drains the balance you forgot it could touch.

◳

Liquidity that isn't there

The pool shows depth. Most of it is owned by one address that can withdraw the moment you're in, leaving you holding a token you cannot sell.

◰

Impostor contracts

Same name, same logo, address off by four characters. Nothing on the confirmation screen distinguishes it from the real one.

◲

Silent price drift

The quoted rate diverges from every other market. Without an independent oracle to compare against, the number looks perfectly reasonable.

The gap

Every one of these is detectable before signing — the evidence is on-chain and in market data. What's missing is something that reads it in the two seconds before you click, and answers in words instead of metrics.

How it works

Four steps, then an answer.

1

You supply a target

A contract, a wallet, or a pending transaction. Nothing else is required — no connection, no signature.

2

Flare data is gathered

FTSOv2 for independent prices, the Data Connector for attested external facts, chain state for the contract's own history.

3

Factors are scored

Each risk factor is evaluated separately and weighted, so a single severe finding can't be averaged away by benign ones.

4

You get a verdict

Proceed, caution, or stop — with the specific reasons listed, ranked by how much each one moved the result.

Weights are published, not hidden

A score you can't interrogate is just an opinion with a number attached. Every factor, its weight, and its contribution to the final result are visible in the output.

Severe findings override the average. A contract that can mint without limit is not rescued by having deep liquidity — that factor alone caps the verdict at stop.
